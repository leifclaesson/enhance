What it actually does

Television lied to you. This one doesn’t.

Every crime drama that ever “enhanced” a licence plate out of four blurry pixels was inventing detail that was never there. ENHANCE!! does the honest version: it magnifies what is genuinely on your screen, pixel-for-pixel, and never pretends there was more.

⚲

Follow-cursor magnifier

Park the window anywhere; it shows the neighbourhood around your mouse, blown up 2× to 64× on the wheel. Straight off the GPU — zero CPU copies, and ~0% load when the screen sits still.

▣

Clean nearest-neighbour

Exact, square pixels through a point sampler — the whole reason it exists. The built-in Windows Magnifier samples bilinear and smears; this stays razor-crisp at every zoom.

⊞

Pixel inspector chrome

A crosshair box marks the inspected pixel, an optional grid separates the magnified blocks, and a HUD shows the live hex / RGB readout and on-screen coordinates under the cursor.

■

Copy the colour

Grab the pixel under the cursor as C hex or Shift+C rgb() — the cursor stays put on the target. The HUD swatch shows it live without copying at all.

◻

Capture a region

Right-click to grab a rectangle to the clipboard — either the raw 1:1 pixels or the blown-up nearest-neighbour ENHANCE!! look with the grid baked in. Click the wordmark to re-grab with fresh pixels.

⚘

Inspector Hue

Drag a region and it freezes zoomed-to-fill as a still pixel inspector: scroll to zoom, drag to pan, hover for RGB, click any pixel to copy its hex. The detective watches from the corner.

The honest part

On TV, “enhance” conjures detail out of nothing. Here it’s the opposite promise: what you see was already there.

The magnifier is a point sampler, not an upscaler. It never interpolates, never guesses a sub-pixel, never paints a face into a reflection. When you zoom 64×, you get 64× wider blocks of the exact pixels the GPU handed it — and the crosshair readout is locked to the true cursor pixel, not the eased background.

It even goes the other way for honesty: a legacy app bitmap-stretched by the OS to 200 / 300 / 400% gets decimated back down to its true pixels on a “capture original” grab, and a fractionally scaled window earns a plain hint that the blur is app-side, not a thing it can un-blur.
